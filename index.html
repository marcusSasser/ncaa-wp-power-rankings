College Win Probability Model

Methodology: For both Division I college football and Division I men's college basketball each winning team receives a score based on win probability control (average and volatility), which is then stacked into a percentile for the teams "score." With those individual scores, an SRS system is run to get the final results. Pretty simple with the goal to create a "more effective" NET rating system by looking at game flow more than just the final score. To see the ratings, check below. Results can be interpreted as the percentile victory a team would be expected to have against an average opponent. While it may lead to shaky results early on, the preseason data used is your final ranking from last year and it decomposes in equal increments until 7 games have been played for CFB and 17 for CBB, when it is completely phased out. Largely this is okay, but there will be outliers (see: Florida State football). There is also an artifical cap placed on how positive the result a team can have against vastly inferior opponents, thanks to the percentile implementation. This is not much of an issue, but can, on occasion, make for blowout wins where a team actually loses value. Additionally, all games played by teams in the DI level count, requiring the use of other models to approximate the skill level of any non-DI opponents. CFB ratings will be updated every Tuesday throughout the season and CBB ratings whenever I get around to it, but at least every Monday. The last point is that injuries are not accounted for in any way and all games, no matter when in the season, receive equal weight.

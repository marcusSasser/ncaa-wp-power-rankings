Win Probability College Model

Explainer: For both Division I college football and Division I men's college basketball each game receives a score based on win probability control (average and volatility), which is then

stacked into a percentile which is the teams "score." With those individual scores, an SRS system is ran to get the final results. Pretty simple with the goal to create a more effective

NET rating system by looking to game flow more than just the final score. To see the ratings, check below. Results can be interpreted as the percentile victory a team would be expected

to have against an average team. While it may lead to shaky results early on, the preseason data used is your final ranking from last year and it decomposes in equal increments until

7 games have been played for CFB and 17 for CBB. Largely this is okay, but there will be outliers (see: Florida State football). There is also an artifical cap placed on how positive the

result a team can have against vastly inferior opponents, thanks to the percentile implementation. This is not much of an issue, but can, on occasion, make for blowout wins where a team

actually loses value. Additionally, all games played by teams in the DI level count, requiring the use of other models to approximate the skill level of any non-DI opponents.
